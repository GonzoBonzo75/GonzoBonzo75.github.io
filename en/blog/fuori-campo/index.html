Out of frame

11 November 2026

Everything that lies outside the frame. A photograph is, first of all, a choice about what to leave out.

The viewfinder’s rectangle cuts. On the left there was a policeman, on the right a woman crying, behind the photographer there was the rest of the world. Whoever looks sees only the inside, and tends to believe it is everything: a photograph looks like proof, and proof is not up for discussion.

That is why two photographers in the same spot, in the same minute, can come home with two opposite stories. A tense square can become a charge or a celebration, depending on where the edge was set. It is not lying: it is a limit of the medium, and also its strength.

But the out-of-frame does not disappear. It stays in the memory of those who were there, and sooner or later someone asks: “And what was next to it?” That question is to be expected, not avoided.

What can be done is to know it. And to say it, in the caption, in the text, in a sequence that widens the view. A good photograph does not claim to show everything: it declares the point from which it is looking.
