The Sheet and the Flame

9 November 2026

Some things weigh more than the paper they are written on.

A bill, for instance: a sheet folded in a pocket, a number that makes you lower your eyes at the end of the month. And then, for a moment, a flame that takes it and gives it back to the sky.

Behind every bill there is a kitchen. A table, a drawer where the papers are kept, someone who opens them last in the evening, when the children are asleep, and puts them away without a word.

In the figures Istat published in October, for 2025, more than 5.4 million people live in absolute poverty, about 2.1 million families, and among them more than a million minors. They haven’t increased on the year before, and perhaps that is exactly the point: hardship is no longer news, it has become landscape. Nor are they only families without work: where the person bringing home a wage is a blue-collar worker, more than one family in seven is poor.

These are families who have learned to do the sums before switching on a light, to put off a check-up, to say “another day” to a child who asks for a trip. Families who hold each other up, and who in this suspended time find themselves asking not how much to spend, but how much longer they can hold out.

On 7 November, in Turin, on Corso Regina Margherita, I saw one of those bills burn. In one hand the sheet in flames, in the other a flare spitting sparks: on one side the weight, on the other the light.

Around her, the light fading between the branches and a row of raised phones, all stealing the same second.

I can’t say what is right to ask for, or from whom. I can only say what I saw: a small, almost domestic gesture, carried into the street in front of everyone, as if to show what is usually kept in a drawer.

I was in the middle of it, looking for the moment when anger looks like a celebration and the celebration, if you look closely, looks like a prayer.
