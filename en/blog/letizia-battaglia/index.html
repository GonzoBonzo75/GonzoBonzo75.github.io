Letizia Battaglia

14 November 2026

On Saturdays, a photographer. The first is a woman.

Letizia Battaglia was born in Palermo in 1935. In 1971 she left her marriage, with three daughters to raise, and started taking photographs: at first because an article with pictures sold better.

In 1974 she came back to Palermo and went to work at L’Ora, the city’s left-wing daily. She stayed until it closed in 1992. In those years Palermo’s crime news passed in front of her lens: the murdered in the street, the funerals, the arrests. Hundreds of thousands of photographs.

She looked at what many in the city preferred not to see. She did it in black and white, up close, without turning away.

In 1985 she won the award named after W. Eugene Smith, the same Smith as the Minamata photograph I didn’t show you: she was the first European woman to receive it. The same year she joined Palermo’s city council; later she would also sit in the Sicilian Regional Assembly.

She did not stop photographing to do something else. She understood that the same thing drove her, with a camera in her hands or without one.

She founded a workshop for young photographers, edited a magazine made entirely by women and in 2017, at the Cantieri Culturali alla Zisa in Palermo, brought to life the International Centre of Photography, in an old workshop pavilion. She died on 13 April 2022, aged 87.

When I think of her I think of something simple: looking is not enough, you also have to decide which side you are on. She did it in the city where she was born, with things happening right outside her door. For those who do my job, it is an uncomfortable and necessary reminder.

Below, one of her photographs: the arrest of the boss Leoluca Bagarella, Palermo, 1980. A man with handcuffs on his wrists, between two men in uniform, in the harsh light of the flash. His gaze goes outside the frame. Nothing more is needed.
