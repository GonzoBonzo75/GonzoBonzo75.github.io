The contact sheet

18 November 2026

On Wednesdays, a short one. This one is about the contact sheet.

The contact sheet used to be a sheet of paper: every frame of a roll printed small, side by side. You went over it with a loupe and a grease pencil, and marked the good ones.

The paper is gone, the gesture isn’t. After a job, the first thing I do is look at everything small, all at once. Every folder in my archive has its own contact sheet.

At a small size a photograph cannot lean on detail. Either it holds up through composition, or it doesn’t. It is an honest filter.

The contact sheet also shows the rhythm: where I insisted, where I doubted, where I shot the same scene three times because I didn’t yet know what I was looking for.

Looking at a single photograph, full screen, is the quickest way to fall in love with it. The contact sheet does the opposite: it puts it next to the others, and sometimes cuts it down to size.

That is why the choice comes after. First you look at the sequence: the story, almost always, is there and not in the single frame.

Try it with a body of your own work: put them all small on the same page and see where your eye stops.
