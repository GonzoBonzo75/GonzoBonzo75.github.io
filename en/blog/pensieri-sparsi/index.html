Scattered thoughts

16 November 2026

On Mondays, scattered thoughts. They have no order, and that’s fine: they are what comes to mind when the camera stays in the bag.

The best photograph often arrives a second after the one you were waiting for. You already knew the one you were waiting for. The other, you didn’t.

How long you stay matters more than how much you shoot. Whoever arrives, takes three pictures and leaves brings home what they expected to find.

With a 28 millimetre lens you can’t cheat: to fill the frame you have to get closer, and getting closer costs. It costs a permission, a bit of fear, a bit of embarrassment. That is why close photographs have another voice.

In a packed square, at some point you stop hearing the noise. That is when you find out whether you are still looking or merely reacting.

Working as a photo editor taught me more than many years of shooting. It forces you to say no, and to explain why.

Blur is not always a mistake, and sharpness is not always a virtue.

After a long job, the most useful thing is to look at nothing for a day. The good photographs won’t run away. The bad ones, the next day, are easier to see.

If one of these thoughts comes back to you in a few days, it was the right one.
