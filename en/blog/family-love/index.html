Family Love

13 November 2026

On Fridays, a book. The first is “Family Love” by Darcy Padilla, which I keep at home and open again every now and then for no particular reason. My copy carries her signature on the title page.

On 28 February 1993, in the lobby of the Ambassador Hotel in San Francisco, Padilla meets an eighteen-year-old girl holding an eight-day-old baby. Their names are Julie Baird and Rachel. Padilla is there to photograph AIDS patients housed in the neighbourhood’s low-cost residence hotels, and Julie has just learned that she is HIV-positive.

It was meant to be a reportage. It becomes a life: Padilla keeps photographing Julie and her family for almost eighteen years, from the streets of San Francisco to Alaska, where Julie dies in September 2010.

The book came out in 2014, in Paris, from La Martinière, with texts by Emmanuel Carrère and Christian Caujolle. The work has received, among other recognitions, the W. Eugene Smith Grant: the same Smith I wrote about on Tuesday.

What strikes me is not the harshness of the images but the time. Today we photograph quickly and move on. Here time is the very form of the story: a photographer who stays, year after year, while her subject’s life gets worse, sometimes better, and then ends.

It is not a book to leaf through quickly. Page after page you realise that the subject was neither the illness nor poverty, but a young woman and her children. Perhaps staying with a person over time is the highest form of respect a photographer can offer.
