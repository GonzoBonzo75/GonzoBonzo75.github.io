The Bang Bang Club

15 November 2026

On Sundays, a film. The first is about us: those who go where the shooting is with a camera around their neck.

The Bang Bang Club, by Steven Silver, 2010. It tells of four photographers, Greg Marinovich, Kevin Carter, Ken Oosterbroek and João Silva, in the townships of South Africa between Nelson Mandela’s release in 1990 and the first free elections of 1994. These are the years when apartheid ends and violence does not: clashes between factions, police, the dead in the streets.

The film is based on the book two of them, Marinovich and Silva, wrote together: Snapshots from a Hidden War.

These are true stories, and they weigh. Marinovich won a Pulitzer in 1991 for his photographs of a killing in 1990. Carter won his in 1994, for the photograph of a little girl in Sudan with a vulture behind her. We will talk about that photograph here, on a Tuesday, with the calm it deserves.

Ken Oosterbroek died on 18 April 1994 in Thokoza, during a firefight. Carter died in July of the same year. João Silva, in October 2010, a few weeks after the film’s premiere, lost both legs below the knee to a landmine in Afghanistan.

It is not a perfect film. Reviews were lukewarm, and Marinovich himself distanced himself from it. Some reproach it for making war photographers look like cold outsiders, come to take and leave.

But it asks the right question: when do you lower the camera? Anyone in this job carries it around, even when it is not said out loud. There is no answer valid for everyone: there is the moment when you choose.

Watch it with that question in your pocket. And sooner or later, on a Tuesday, we will come back to the little girl and the vulture.
